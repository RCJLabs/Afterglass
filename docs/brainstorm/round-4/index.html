Where it stands

Everything on this page comes from an autopilot (a scripted player, 40 to 200 seeds a result) and scripted runs in Chromium. Nobody has played it yet.

The slice's question is unanswered.

It exists to ask do people want to play a second season? The page asks at the end of a season and saves the answer in the playtest export, but no tester has seen it.

The night no longer has to collapse onto the line's floor, but working below it doesn't pay.

The static line no longer wins: over 1,000 seeds the reacting plan and a doubled line tie on the two-room start. The best play kept nearly everyone on the line's floor, because a room lit below it took a whole tide alone. The Unlit now take any dark way up and gnaw only a light that bars every way. Working the rooms below the line on one side of the keep now costs about 2 points of second seasons on either keep, against 10.6 on the two-room start before: a near-even trade, not a mistake, and not a gain.

Both seasons may be too easy.

The autopilot finishes 95–98% of first seasons, and 90–98% of the second seasons it reaches on either keep, now that the second is a summer. A whole year is harder: 23–45% of keeps come through winter and the Long Night. People will do worse than a script that knows the rules, but by how much is unknown.

The dawn rite still isn't shown to be a choice.

Traits make each shade different, but an autopilot that reads them does no better than one blind to them. The rite is meant to be the game's best decision; whether it feels like one needs people.

The new moon turns on a couple of candles.

When a room a Maw broke also lost its day's work, the balanced plan fell from 95% to 67% of first seasons on the four-floor keep. Almost all of that was the Chandlery, broken the night before the new moon.

Colour blindness is checked only in simulation.

Red eyes on a dark night all but vanished for anyone who can't see red, and a Creeper in the dark is nothing but its eyes. Each Unlit eye now has a pale glint that stays bright whatever the colour vision. No colour-blind player has tried it.

A second season counts only if the first was finished too. "Doubled line": two fighters on each stair up to the Veil on every Maw night, never moved. "Balanced": reacts to tides and Maws.

The night's collapse, and a partial fix

A room lit below the line stood alone across the Unlit's way up. A candle lights 40 of a room's 48 steps, and the way up runs between stairs through the middle of each room, so no candle there stays off it. A Creeper cut off by the line gnawed the first light on its shortest way, so one shade below took a whole tide. Rewards didn't help: up to double the night's work a floor down, or a day bonus for rooms worked through the night, left working below the line 13–17 second seasons behind in about 197. Neither did retreating to the line for each tide, or holding the line a floor lower. What helped was where the Unlit gnaw: they now take any dark way up, however long, and gnaw only a light that bars every way. It's a switch in Settings, on by default.

The balanced plan throughout. "One side below the line": it also works the rooms below the line on the Choir's side of the keep, leaving the other side dark. Working below the line now costs about 2 points on either keep (1.8 standard errors pooled, probably a small real cost), where it cost 10.6 on the two-room start. Nothing below the line clearly pays except the Choir. The rule may also make the game about a point harder (1 standard error on each keep).

The first Maws

They tore down the candle holding the line. That made the line cost something, but a doubled line beat them outright: 96 first seasons in 100 against 95, and 0.2 shades lost a season against 2.4.

An easier second season

At ×1.35 difficulty a season, the second finished 35% of the time. At ×1.2, with the Maws on their own multiplier, it's 73% for the balanced plan on the two-room start.

Maws that break rooms

They weigh the candle barring the way against every room where people work, count the fighters on the way, and go for the most worth for the least fight. A broken room loses its night's work and costs 1 Dread.

On the four-floor keep that reversed the static line's lead. On the two-room start it didn't at first: the first floor you build is always the line's floor, so a doubled line stands in working rooms.

An autopilot that plays sensibly

The reacting plan had been pulling reinforcements across a dark floor for each tide, and posting shades in rooms below the line's floor. Fixing both levelled it with the doubled line on the two-room start and put it ahead on the four-floor keep. The static line's lead was my autopilot's, not the rules'.

What's left is a design problem: on a built-up keep the night is all about the line's floor.

Guarding the line

A rule that shades in the light at the stairs do no work but keep the Watch. It took the balanced plan from 92% to 73% of first seasons and barely moved the doubled line, from 98% to 95%. It's off, as a switch in Settings.

Building

Stone from masons in the Yard, 6 stone a room, three workers a room, a Forge and a Cellar, and a keep that starts as the Hearth and the Crypt. The autopilot's results depend heavily on its build order: swapping the Chapel and the Chandlery once took it from 93% to 30%.

Traits (2 and 4)

Everyone has one of eight; death turns it over. An autopilot that reads traits finished 178 second seasons of 193, one blind to them 175: no measurable difference. On the current build it's 182 of 198 against 185 of 195, still within noise.

I first reported a lead of about 10 under a harsher Dread and made it the default. It came from an autopilot mistake, spending the new moon's essence on the tides of nights 5 and 6, and vanished once fixed; the default went back. The same mistake had put the doubled line ahead by 13–15. With it fixed, Double led by 10 in about 195, and over 1,000 seeds the two tie.

The follow-ups

The doubled line, over 1,000 seeds on the two-room start: the balanced plan finished 932 of 982 second seasons (94.9%), Double 938 of 990 (94.7%). A tie; the lead of 10 in 200 was noise.

Raids, back from 75% to 86% held (90% without traits). The Gentle guarded at half strength and the Greedy ate double; now the Gentle grieve harder and the Greedy work ×0.8 away from their two rooms.

A warning for the new moon. On the two dusks before it, the Dusk panel says what warding every stair against the Hollow will cost against the essence in store. The autopilot's mistake is an easy one for a person too.

The crossing, the guide, the Book, installing

They work in scripted runs: the crossing animates, the guide's cards fire, the Book tells every death, and the app installs and plays offline in Chromium. None of it has been tried by a person.

Decisions only you can make

The first two stand between the slice and its playtest.

Play it on a real phone.

Installing, real fingers, the guide's clarity and iOS are all unchecked. Your notes should decide what gets fixed before testers see it.

How testers send results back.

Today they copy a block of JSON from Records, then Playtest. That works for three to five people; a form or an email link would lose fewer answers beyond that.

Google Play.

An installed app with no URL bar needs assetlinks.json at rcjlabs.github.io/.well-known/: an rcjlabs.github.io repository or a custom domain, and the signing fingerprint from your Play Console.

Settled since the list was made: the Maws keep their name. They break rooms now, as round three's Maws do.

The queue

The candidates not built yet when the queue was made, in the order I'd build them. The first two help the playtest itself; the next two matter once there are players; the rest add to the game.

  1. A threat preview at dusk (16), with the black mirror (6) in it. M

    Done. The Dusk panel's black mirror says where each rift's tide will go past the candles set so far: the light it will gnaw, a mirror left open, or a shade it will catch. It also names where candle hunters go, what a Maw would make for, the Hollow's way, each tide's size, and tomorrow's raid as a range. The Tain shows the same ways as marching chevrons. It asks the Creepers' own planner, so it's exact until the night begins and candles burn down.

  2. Watch a replay (15). S–M

    Parked for later, your call. A tester's export already replays exactly. A viewer that plays it back lets you see where they struggled, which is most of what a playtest can tell beyond its one question.

  3. A season recap card (13). S

    Done. At a season's end, or when the keep falls, Make the card draws a 1080 by 1350 image. It shows the keep as it stands in the game's own pixels, how the season went, six numbers, and up to three of the season's dead from the Book. Share uses the phone's share sheet where the browser can share images; Save image downloads it. Only the download is tested (desktop Chromium has no share sheet), and nobody has shared one yet.

  4. A daily seed (14). S

    Everyone plays the same keep each day. Worth it once there are players to compare.

  5. Break a mirror in an emergency (8). S

    Done. Everyone in it goes free at once and Dread falls 1 for each, so it can turn a censure into a warning, or free a caught shade at night. The mirror is lost and 7 days of bad luck follow (sickness twice as often). The keeper plan breaking one to stop every censure finished 80 second seasons in 99 against 86 without; without the bad luck it's about even.

  6. Fire by day (the rest of 2). M

    Done. About 2 fires a season in a Hearth or a Forge. The room's own workers fight each one, the Yard or the bell can be sent, and fighting it can kill, more so the hotter it is. Fought at once it adds few deaths (0.14–0.18 per two seasons for the autopilot) but costs 5–14 second seasons in 200. Whether it gives a slower player more choices at the rite is for the playtest.

  7. Quarters and the Dreamwell; Weepers (5). M

    Done. The keep sleeps 8, and each Quarters 4 more; the crowded fall sick more often. A shade dreaming in the Dreamwell makes the living work ×1.1 the next day. The night after a death a Weeper rises for each of the dead and weeps in the dark of the sleepers' twin; one that weeps its fill gives someone a nightmare (×0.6 work). It doesn't pull the night from the line as hoped. The Quarters goes where the keep builds next, the Tain's deepest floor, so the sleepers' twin is always below the line. Ignored, the Weepers give about 6 nightmares in two seasons. On the two-room start that costs nothing measurable over two seasons; on the four-floor keep it costs the doubled line 22 second seasons in 200, for reasons I haven't found. A fighter sent to guard costs 16 second seasons in 200, and a dreamer comes out even. Never building the Quarters did as well as building it when crowded, or better. For now they add texture, not a decision.

  8. Whispers and the great glass (7). M

    Done. By day a shade can whisper its old trade to whoever works it now (×1.25), or step through a great glass and work a room in person. Either costs it 8 memory at dusk. At 4 memory whispering was a free gain on a built-up keep (+12 second seasons in 200), so not a decision. At 8 it's +8 there and about even on a new keep. The autopilot never saves the 30 glass a great glass costs. Given it at the start, stepping through added 3 and 9 second seasons.

  9. A full year (10). L

    Done. The seasons run spring, summer, autumn and winter. Summer's days are ×1.3 as long, and so is a day's work, with nights ×0.75; winter is the reverse, ×0.7 and ×1.35, longer than a candle burns. Winter's seventh night is the Long Night, which ends the year. Summer and autumn came out easier than springs at the same hardness. Winter took 29–46% of the keeps that reached it before the Long Night, and the Long Night a quarter to nearly half of the rest: 23–45% of keeps survive a year, against 42–55% with every season a spring. Round three's double-length Long Night was won 13 times in 109, whatever its Creepers, so it lasts 1.5 winter nights. I built this before the playtest, which the plan had waited for. It also makes the playtest's second season a summer, which is easier.
