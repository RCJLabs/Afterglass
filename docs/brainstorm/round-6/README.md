# Round six: the nights, and seven more (September 2026)

Round five's queue is built, and so are the two follow-ups after it: the Lantern Church's crusade and a softer Hard ([`round-5/`](../round-5/README.md)). This round has ten candidates. Three are for the nights, as you asked; the other seven are my picks. Sizes are my estimates: S, M, L. Where a line says measured, it's the autopilot, not people.

**Why the nights need it.** The plan that sets its line at dusk and never moves anyone, Double, comes through 97 whole years in 200. The balanced plan, which reacts all night, comes through 82 ([problem 16](../../../prototype/README.md)). So the night rewards setting up well and then watching. The three night items are aimed at that. Each one is measured by whether the reacting plans gain on Double, not just whether everyone does better.

**Three need you.** Item 4 needs testers who've never seen the game. Item 5 needs a domain and your Play Console account. Item 10 needs your call on whether the campaign replaces the open-ended year or sits beside it.

## The nights

1. **Shade abilities: one act a night.** Today a shade stands in its light, moves when told, and fights what reaches it. Give each kind one act a night, paid in its own memory (10 to 15), so spending a shade you're about to let go is a real choice:
   - **Loyal: Stand.** For 10 seconds nothing gets past its light, however many come: a stair held through a surge.
   - **Serene: Kindle.** Relights the nearest snuffed candle within reach, or makes its own burn to the end of the night.
   - **Pale: Pass unseen.** For 15 seconds the Unlit ignore it: out into the dark and back.
   - **Stranger: Lure.** For 8 seconds the Unlit within reach come for it, pulling a tide off a weak stair at its own risk.

   Traits bend them: a Reckless shade's Stand strikes back, and a Coward's Pass lasts longer. A button on the bar when a shade is picked, and a key. The autopilot uses them by rule, and the guide teaches them on night 2.
2. **Lanterns, and something worth fetching.** Round three answered the Hollow with "lanterns, the whole Watch, the hush", and lanterns were never built.
   - **Light that moves.** A shade can carry a lantern: a small light of its own that burns a candle's wax at twice the rate. It walks the dark safely while the wax lasts, and the Hollow goes for lanterns first.
   - **What's worth carrying it to.** Each night one or two things turn up in dark rooms:
     - an echo, a lost memory: +20 memory to the shade that brings it back to a mirror;
     - a relic from the Deep: glass or quicksilver;
     - a sleepwalker from the Quarters who has wandered into the Tain: walk them back before a Creeper finds them, or they die in their sleep and wake Pale.
   - **Seen at dusk.** The black mirror shows them, so fetching is a plan, not a surprise. It has to pay sometimes and cost sometimes; the autopilot fetches by rule, and I compare that against leaving them.
3. **Omens: each night its own shape, and a clock to read it by.** Every night now differs mostly in how many come. An omen changes where the danger is.
   - **The omen.** At dusk the black mirror shows tonight's omen. Some nights it offers two, each with a price and a prize. About eight, for example:
     - a sealed rift, so everything comes up the other one;
     - a thin Veil: seepers from the first tide;
     - the Hunt: two Maws, each worth 3 essence cut down;
     - still air: candles burn half as fast;
     - a blood moon: half again as many Creepers, each worth essence;
     - clear sky: the black mirror shows every tide to the second;
     - the Deep breathes: the rifts open somewhere else for the night. That one's the hardest to build.
   - **The tide clock.** Along the top of the night, the tides, the Maw, the Hollow and dawn show as marks. A Skip button runs to the next mark while nothing is happening, and stops the moment something does, for the quiet stretches of a two-minute night on a phone.
   - **Measured:** each omen's cost to the autopilot, tuned so choosing between two is a real choice.

## Enhancements

4. **The playtest kit, with the replay viewer.** Most open questions in the README end "untested with people". Round five's first caveat was that the tutorial needs two or three players who've never seen the game.
   - **A tester link.** It opens the tutorial on a fresh slot with a short note. At the end of day 3, or whenever they stop, it asks three questions and offers one button to send the session back, as a file to share or text to copy.
   - **The replay viewer** (round four's #15, parked as your call). Load an export and watch it at 1× to 16×, day and night. The moments that mattered are marked (deaths, cracks, catches), and so is where the tester paused, opened a panel or sat idle. The sim already replays exactly; what's new is recording those page events in the export, and the viewer itself.
   - **No server,** so sessions come back by hand.
5. **Onto Google Play.** The season installs as a web app now. As a Trusted Web Activity it needs Digital Asset Links at the root of its domain, which a project site can't serve ([problem 18](../../../prototype/README.md)).
   - **Your part:** either an `rcjlabs.github.io` repository for the organisation, or a custom domain; and the Play Console account and the upload.
   - **My part:** the Bubblewrap build, the asset-links file, the store listing (the icons exist, the scripted checks can take the screenshots, a feature graphic), a privacy line (nothing leaves the phone), and an offline checklist for a real device.
6. **A balance pass on what measured weak.** Six things the numbers flagged and I left:
   - fog costs the autopilot nothing, but only because it never reads the black mirror, so measuring it needs an autopilot that does ([problem 27](../../../prototype/README.md));
   - generations change who lives in the keep, not whether it lasts (29);
   - hiding a mirror from a crusade saves mirrors, not keeps (31);
   - the weather costs Double most, about 5 whole years in 200, and lets the Hollow through more often (27);
   - Hard's second year is nearly unwinnable, 2 of 26 second winters (30);
   - a tall keep is a trap: always starting a new floor cost the balanced plan 31 first seasons in 200 (23).

   Each gets a change, a measurement and a README line. It belongs after the three night items, which will move every table anyway.

## New features

7. **Visitors at the gate: the day's choices.** Round three planned about sixty events. The day has about six kinds: sickness, old age, fire, a newcomer, a raid and the Church. Add about twenty visitors, each with two or three answers and a cost you can see. For example:
   - a glass peddler: food for glass, or send him on;
   - pilgrims before a raid: more hands, and more mouths;
   - a grave-robber caught in the crypt: hanged, he wakes as a Restless shade; let go, he takes a body with him;
   - a knight asking after a brother among your dead: release the Stranger he names, or keep it;
   - the plague cart: turn it away for Dread, or take it in and risk the sickness;
   - a wedding, a bard, a deserter from the Host.

   One or two a day at most, in the Day panel with a clock. The autopilot answers by rule, so what each one costs is measurable.
8. **The Library, the Hall and the Gatehouse.** Round three had twelve room pairs; these three aren't built.
   - **The Library, twin the Archive of the Dead: research.** Remembrance buys about eight improvements a keep: candles that burn longer, cheaper wards, stronger pitch, a second act a night for one kind. A shade in the Archive by night speeds the next.
   - **The Hall, twin the Court of Shades.** One standing decree a season (rationing, a curfew, a levy), each with a price. The Court hears the dead's requests, and a shade seated there answers one free.
   - **The Gatehouse.** By day it holds the gate: guards in it count for more. Siege ladders, round three's other raid tool and still unbuilt, need its walls. Round three's twin for it "faces the Deep; big incursions come here": a third way up under it in later seasons. Round three called that twin the Maw, a name the Unlit now have, so it needs another. A stronger gate by day buys a new weak point by night, which is the game's own idea.
9. **The eclipse.** Round three's set piece, cut for cost. Once a year, at midsummer, day and night run at once for a few minutes: the living at work and the dead in the Tain, the Host at the gate while the Unlit climb. The screen shows both halves, the keep above and the Tain below, as the Veil does. A living person and their dead fight side by side, and anyone who dies during it wakes at once. Round three called it "the set piece that proves the two halves belong together". The risk is the phone: half a screen for each.
10. **A campaign: five years, five chapters.** Round three's plan was a five-year campaign, then a sandbox: The First Winter, The Ashen Host, The Lantern Church, The Deep Rises, The Long Night. Today a year ends and the next is the same, harder.
    - **Each year a chapter:** a goal, a new pressure brought in (sieges and ladders in year 2, the Church's escalation in year 3, the Hollow growing in year 4), and a closing choice.
    - **Year 5** ends in round three's three endings, including the one that was cut: Open the Veil, where the living and the dead share both realms.
    - **It teaches, too:** the game could arrive a chapter at a time rather than all by the first autumn.
    - **Last in the queue,** because it's the item that ties the others together.
